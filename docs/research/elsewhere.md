## Kasuri, in the tradition of Kurume

- "The warp and weft threads are resist-dyed in specific patterns prior to weaving", the bundles "tied or bound by hand". Three kinds: tate gasuri (warp dyed), yoko gasuri (weft dyed) and tate-yoko gasuri (both). Patterns have "a blurred or brushed appearance". Checked: [Wikipedia: Kasuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasuri). The article flags its own warp and weft naming as contradictory in one section.
- Kurume kasuri was designated an Important Intangible Cultural Property in 1957; certification requires hand-tied yarn, natural indigo and a hand loom. Sourced, snippet only: [Kurume City](https://www.city.kurume.fukuoka.jp/1080kankou/2015bunkazai/3050kurumeshishi/files/No.21KurumeKasuri.pdf). Dates differ between sources.
- No restricted or sacred designs found. Whether "Kurume Kasuri" is a protected name is unchecked, so our cloth is called "kasuri in the tradition of Kurume", with our own motifs.
- The origin of the name from *kasureru*, "blurred", was not supported by the source given; left out.
- **For the code:** each thread's colour changes along its length (white where it was tied, indigo elsewhere), and each thread is shifted slightly from where it should be, so motif edges blur.
- **Caption:** In kasuri the threads are tied and dyed with indigo before weaving, so the pattern only forms as each thread meets its neighbours in the loom, with slightly blurred edges. Kurume, in Kyushu, is one of its best-known homes.
- **Credit:** weavers of the Kurume kasuri tradition, Fukuoka.

## Kente, Asante and Ewe

- Narrow strips are woven on a double-heddle loom and sewn edge to edge; a V&A Ewe cloth has 19 strips, each about 10 cm by 280 cm. Sourced: [V&A O164606](https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O164606/kente-textile-unknown).
- A warp-faced plain-weave ground with blocks of extra weft patterning; sources disagree on how warp- or weft-faced Asante cloth is. Sourced, partly snippet only: [Met 320678](https://metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/320678), [Wikipedia: Kente cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kente_cloth).
- **The law.** Under Ghana's Copyright Act 2005, rights in folklore are "vested in the President on behalf of, and in trust for, the people of Ghana", and the definition "specifically includes Kente and Adinkra designs whose authors are unknown". Kente became Ghana's first geographical indication in 2025, so only qualifying producers may use the name in trade. Checked: [Afriwise](https://www.afriwise.com/blog/kente-as-intellectual-property-is-ghanas-dual-protection-framework-fit-for-purpose).
- **Permission.** The National Folklore Board asks people to "seek permission from the Board before using expressions of folklore outside the customary context and or for commercial purposes"; personal use, news and education are exempt. Checked: [GNA, 2023](https://gna.org.gh/?p=292006). Whether an artwork showing generated strip structure needs permission is unclear.
- Some patterns are customarily reserved for the Asantehene. Sourced from secondary sources only.
- **For the code:** a cloth made of strips, each with its own warp stripes and blocks of weft floats, laid side by side and offset.
- **Recommendation:** write to the National Folklore Board before showing anything; use only our own strip layouts from the structure, never a named pattern.

## Dumbara, Kandy

- UNESCO inscribed the craft of Dumbara mats in 2021: "The mats are made in two villages (Kalasirigama and Alokagama) by a community called kinnara", from hana fibre, "decorated with symbolic motifs and designs"; the weaver "pulls the lath through the yarns on the loom to create the desired motif". Checked: [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-craftsmanship-of-making-dumbara-rat-kalla-01693).
- Dumbara cotton cloth (extra weft on a two-treadle loom) is a related but separate tradition. Sourced, snippet only: [University of Moratuwa](https://dl.lib.uom.lk/items/b9605b38-7882-43fe-8453-568475b825c1/full).
- The motifs carry Buddhist or Hindu meaning, and no published motif names, drawings or meanings were found. So no motifs are invented or borrowed until there is a named source, perhaps the Sri Lankan design book Eleanor has.
- **For the code:** a plain-weave ground with pattern wefts floating over chosen warp threads in each row; the same extension kente needs.
