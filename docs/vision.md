# Vision

The working name is **Light Weaver**.

## In one sentence

Threads of light weaving themselves into cloth, row by row, in the patterns of heritage textiles from around the world, generated from each tradition's own weaving rules, made to be watched and eventually projected onto real things.

## Where it comes from

**Todd Moyer's Illoominated II**, a projection and light installation first shown at the 2025 Portland Winter Light Festival: giant spools appear to unspool laser "threads" that weave into a shifting tapestry. His earlier Illoominated used nine projectors to throw live-generated, non-repeating patterns onto nine surfaces, and his first Illoominate dates from 2014. Sources: [Make](https://makezine.com/article/craft/fine-art/lightweaver-illuminating-museums-and-festivals), [Core77](https://core77.com/posts/135434/An-Eye-Catching-Installation-Lasers-as-Unspooling-Thread), [Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/bmyxvm/illoominated-mixes-giant-spools-of-thread-and-lasers), [Derivative](https://derivative.ca/community-post/todd-moyers-illoominate/60739). Derivative makes TouchDesigner, which suggests the piece was built with it (unchecked).

Light Weaver takes the idea of weaving with light somewhere of its own: the cloth is real weaving, worked out thread by thread from real traditions.

## What it is like

- **Cloth builds up before you.** Warp threads (the lengthwise ones, held on the loom) glow in place; weft threads (the crosswise ones) are carried across one row at a time, going over and under, and the pattern appears as the rows stack up.
- **The pattern is real.** A herringbone tweed is woven the way herringbone is woven; a tartan's stripes cross the way a tartan's do. Nothing is a picture laid on top.
- **Traditions from around the world.** One piece of cloth, then another: a Scottish tartan, Welsh double cloth, Ghanaian kente, a kilim, Japanese kasuri, Sri Lankan Dumbara weaving. Each with its own colours, and perhaps its own way of being woven in light.
- **Passive.** It plays by itself, like the aquarium's scenes or Cat's Eyes.

## Why heritage textiles

They are centuries of pattern-making, each tradition with its own rules, materials and meanings. Generating them from their rules is a way of understanding them, and the [catalogue](ideas.md#a-catalogue-of-traditions) is a way of showing where each one comes from. It links to the Sri Lankan influence in Eleanor's website, and to the history of computing: the Jacquard loom was controlled by punched cards, which inspired early computers (unchecked).

## In scope and out of scope

**Woven textiles only, for now.** Some heritage textiles are woven, but get their pattern from dyeing the threads beforehand (ikat, kasuri); those are in. Others are not woven at all and are out for now (all unchecked): batik (wax-resist dyeing on finished cloth, the best-known Sri Lankan textile craft), Fair Isle and Aran (knitted), phulkari (embroidered), ajrak (block-printed). Knitting is the likeliest extension, as its rules are as strict as weaving's.
